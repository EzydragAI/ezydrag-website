Every CRM now ships an SDR agent. So… what's your edge?

Shuvam Santra, Chief Technology Officer and Anirban Dasgupta, Chief Operating Officer9 Nov 2026 · 2 min read
TL;DR

Salesforce made a lead-nurture agent a standard feature. When everyone has the same agent, the edge is your rules, your content, your approvals and who runs it.

What happened

Right, so, I watched the Dreamforce recap twice, and I want to be fair to it, because there's a lot of genuinely good stuff in there.

Salesforce's recap listed 21 announcements. The one for sales teams: Piper, now generally available, an AI sales development agent that "works around the clock to nurture your inbound prospects." It qualifies leads, books meetings and syncs everything back to the CRM.

Long-horizon agents went to pilot too, with goals like "re-engage my at-risk deals" running over days or weeks inside guardrails you set. General availability was listed for November. They also shipped the boring-but-important stuff: Agent Health Monitoring, and an Agent Optimizer that ranks recurring failures.

55%

of sales professionals already used AI for prospecting, according to Salesforce's own survey in February.

When everyone has the same agent, the agent stops being your advantage.

The tier list

So we made a tier list. I'm aware a tier list is not peer-reviewed. It's still correct.

What we'd tell a founder

  • Own your rules. Who's a good lead, what "ready" means, when to stop emailing.
  • Own your voice. Agents write from your approved content. Thin content, thin emails.
  • Own the approvals. Decide who signs off. Write it down.
  • Make someone responsible for running it. A health dashboard has never fixed anything by itself. Not once.
