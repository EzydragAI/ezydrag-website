Why watching an AI work makes you trust it more (yes, it's psychology)…

Santanu Mondal, Director of Design9 Nov 2026 · 2 min read
TL;DR

People trust what they can see being done. It's called the labor illusion. Agents take minutes, not milliseconds, so show the real work, step by step. Never fake it.

A question for you

You search for flights. One site answers instantly. Another shows you a little progress bar: checking airlines, comparing fares. Both give you the exact same results.

Which one do you trust more?

Be honest. It's the slower one, na? Don't worry. You're not weird. You're human.

We trust the work we can see. Even when the result is identical.

This is called the labor illusion

In 2011, Ryan Buell and Michael Norton published a paper in Management Science called "The Labor Illusion: How Operational Transparency Increases Perceived Value." Their finding: when a website shows people it's working on their request, people value the service more, even when it takes longer.

Their own summary is lovely: "providing information to your customer will improve perceptions, even if total service time is higher."

Psychology corner

Seeing effort feels like care. When a waiter repeats your order back, nothing gets faster. But you relax, because you can see someone understood you. Same thing.

The pizza that taught everyone

Remember the pizza tracker? According to The Hustle, Domino's launched it in 2008. It was, in their words, "essentially a progress bar." Order placed, prep, bake, quality check, out the door.

The pizza didn't get faster. But waiting felt different, because you could see where your pizza was. That's the whole trick.

Researchers took it further. The same Hustle piece describes a cafeteria study where showing diners the kitchen raised their satisfaction, without slowing the service down. Open the kitchen window, and people like the food more.

Now add time limits

Back in 1993, Jakob Nielsen described three response-time limits that still hold:

  • 0.1 seconds feels instant.
  • 1 second keeps your flow of thought intact.
  • 10 seconds is about the limit for keeping attention. After that, people wander off, so you should show when the result will arrive.
Here's the problem

AI agents don't take 0.1 seconds. They take a minute. Sometimes ten. That's far past Nielsen's attention limit, on every single run. If your agent shows a spinner for three minutes, you're not designing a product. You're designing anxiety.

So, what do we design?

Three simple rules we follow at Ezydrag:

  1. Show the real steps. "Checked 3 sources. Scored 82. Drafted email." Real work, in plain words.
  2. Show what's next and who's needed. If a person must approve, say so before they wonder why it stopped.
  3. Never fake it. A fake progress bar is a dark pattern. The labor illusion works because the labor is real. If there's no work to show, just be fast.

Show the work. Don't perform it.

    One-line summary

    People don't trust magic. They trust visible, honest work. Design your agents to show it.
