MCP just deleted its sessions. Your agents will thank you…

Shuvam Santra, Chief Technology Officer9 Nov 2026 · 2 min read
TL;DR

MCP went stateless. Any request can hit any server. It's plumbing news, and plumbing decides whether your agents survive production.

What happened

On 28 July, the Model Context Protocol published a new spec. MCP is how a lot of AI tools talk to other software. In the spec team's own words, MCP is "transforming from a bidirectional stateful protocol into a request/response stateless protocol."

That sentence will not trend. It should.

The most important boring sentence of the year.

What it means, minus the poetry:

  • No more session handshake. Each request carries everything it needs.
  • Servers that need memory hand back a handle. The model passes it along.
  • New headers, so gateways can route and rate-limit without reading the body.
  • Stricter authorization. The team admits it "is where implementers spend most of their integration time." Correct.

Why you should care

Stateful connections are adorable in a demo. In production, a server restarts, a load balancer moves you, and your agent forgets who it was talking to. Stateless means any request can hit any server. That's how normal web software has scaled for twenty years. Agents are finally being held to the same standard.

12 months

The offramp the old HTTP+SSE transport gets before it's gone. Ask your vendors what they'll support after.

What we'd tell a founder

  • Connectors are becoming a commodity. Good. Don't build your edge on plumbing.
  • Your edge is context and rules. What your agent knows about your customers, and what it's allowed to touch.
  • Ask about 2027. If a vendor can't answer, that's your answer.
