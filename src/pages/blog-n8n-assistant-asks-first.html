TL;DR

n8n shipped an agent that builds workflows for you. The impressive part isn't the AI. It's that it asks before it publishes or deletes anything.

What happened

On 9 July, n8n put an AI Assistant in preview. You describe a workflow in plain English. It plans it, builds it, tests it and fixes its own mistakes. What comes out is a normal n8n workflow. You can open it. Read it. Judge it.

I don't get excited about launches. It's not a mood. It's a policy. But two lines in the announcement were correct, and I respect correct.

One: it "asks for confirmation before high-impact actions like publishing, deleting, or other important changes." Two: "your credential and secrets are never sent to the AI."

The AI is the least interesting part of this release.

Why you should care

Every vendor now has an agent that can build things. Fine. Building is cheap. The expensive question is where the agent stops, and almost nobody answers it in the demo.

n8n answered it in the first post. The agent builds. A human ships. The output is something a human can read. Not "trust me."

Plot twist

On 25 September n8n shipped Agents and made it structural: "Mark a tool as sensitive and the agent pauses for Approve or Reject before using it." The confirm button stopped being a feature and became the architecture.

What we'd tell a founder

  • Write down where your agent stops. Before you pick a model. One line per action: who says yes.
  • Make the output readable. A workflow, a draft, a diff. If you can't read it, you can't approve it.
  • Keep secrets out of prompts. The agent calls tools that hold the keys. It never sees them.

How we do it at Ezydrag

Every agent we build has the same shape. It drafts. A named person approves anything that leaves the building. We agree that with a client before we write a line of code. It's boring. Boring is what works on a Tuesday.
