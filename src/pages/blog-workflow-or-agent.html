Workflow or agent? Stop overthinking it. Take the 2-minute test…

Shuvam Santra, Chief Technology Officer and Anirban Dasgupta, Chief Operating Officer9 Nov 2026 · 2 min read
TL;DR

If you can list the steps, it's a workflow. If each step depends on the last answer, it's an agent. Most real jobs are both. Take the quiz below.

What happened

Okay, so, every founder we talk to asks the same question, and I used to give a really long answer. Like, really long. With diagrams. Our Chief Operating Officer has asked me to stop. So here's the short version.

On 25 September, n8n launched Agents. You describe the job, give it a model, tools and workflows, and it figures out the steps. And they compose, which is the part I got genuinely excited about: "Every workflow you've built in n8n is something an agent can use." Mark a tool as sensitive, and the agent pauses "for Approve or Reject before using it."

A week earlier, Atlassian let people @mention agents inside Rovo Chat, so the agent turns up with "the thread, the decisions, and the artifacts it needs." Agents are joining the team chat. Not replacing it.

Turning every if-statement into an autonomous agent is how you build a very expensive coin flip.

The 2-minute test

  • Can you list the steps in advance? Workflow.
  • Does each step depend on the last answer? Agent, with tight tools and an approval before anything leaves.
  • Mixed? Let the workflow drive, and call the agent for the judgement step.

Try it: four real jobs

  1. 1Copy every new webinar lead into Salesforce and tag the campaign.

    You can list every step in advance. Workflow. Cheaper and easier to check.

  2. 2Write the next email for a lead who opened three emails and visited pricing.

    The right email depends on what this lead just did. Agent, with an approval before it sends.

  3. 3Score each lead on fit and intent using rules your sales lead agreed.

    Agreed rules, known steps. Workflow.

  4. 4Read a messy reply and decide whether it's a meeting request, an objection or an out-of-office.

    Each reply is different and the next step depends on it. Agent, with a person reviewing anything unclear.

A real example from sales

Capturing, enriching and scoring a lead: workflow. Writing the right next email for that specific lead: agent. Pressing send: a human. That's exactly how our Sales Nurture Agent is built, and, honestly, it took us a while to get that simple.

Our Chief Operating Officer's hot take

Agents choose their own path, so read their logs the way engineers read code changes. Budget the time for it. It's the cheapest insurance you'll ever buy.
