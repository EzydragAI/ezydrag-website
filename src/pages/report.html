This isn't a survey. It's what a small team noticed while building and running agents for real sales and marketing teams, written down so you can skip a few of our mistakes.

CHAPTER 1

The busywork tax

Ask a marketing ops lead what fills their week and you rarely hear "strategy." You hear lists that arrive in five formats, emails stitched together block by block, last-minute QA, and a quarterly deck rebuilt from spreadsheets.

None of it is hard. All of it is careful, repetitive and easy to get slightly wrong. That combination is exactly where agents help, and exactly where a careless agent hurts.

CHAPTER 2

Context beats prompts

Our early agents had clever prompts and poor results. The model wasn't the problem. The agent simply couldn't see that the contact in Marketo was the same person with an open deal in Salesforce, or that someone promised her pricing in Tuesday's meeting.

An agent is only as good as what it can see.

So we stopped tuning prompts and started connecting things. Accounts, contacts, campaigns, deals, meeting notes and policies now live in one context graph. Agents ask the graph, get a small set of connected facts, and cite where each fact came from.

CHAPTER 3

Approval is a feature, not friction

Fully autonomous agents make good demos and nervous customers. In practice, the teams we work with want the agent to do the work and a person to make the call.

So every send and every sync waits for a named person. That one click turned out to matter more than any model upgrade: it is why teams let agents near real customers at all. Each approval is logged, so "who sent this?" always has an answer.

CHAPTER 4

Tokens are the new cloud bill

Agents that paste whole exports into a prompt are slow and expensive, and the useful signal gets buried. Costs creep up quietly, one agent at a time.

Three habits keep the bill flat: send connected facts instead of exports, answer known questions from approved Q&A with no model call at all, and let an optimization agent watch the others and suggest cuts. You can try the calculator with your own numbers.

CHAPTER 5

Privacy deadlines are product deadlines

India's DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, with full compliance due by 13 May 2027. Many of our clients also serve EU users under GDPR.

The hard part isn't the law. It's knowing where personal data lives. The same graph that helps agents also answers that question, which is why we treat compliance as part of the product rather than a separate audit. More in our compliance guide.

CHAPTER 6

What we're building next

Lead routing and scoring, weekly campaign insights that feed the QBR, inbound inquiry replies, and ongoing data hygiene. Each one starts the same way: one workflow, one owner, one clear measure of done.

If you're running RevOps on Marketo and Salesforce and any of this sounds familiar, we'd like to hear what your week looks like.
